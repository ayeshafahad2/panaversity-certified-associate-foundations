How, when & why each concept is used

These short briefs come before the questions on purpose. Knowing the purpose of a concept makes the right option obvious when you meet it inside a scenario.

Governance

How
Set rules before work starts: approval gates, least-privilege access, what must be reported, and who owns the final decision.
When
Before delegating anything sensitive, whenever data leaves the organisation, or when a new rule arrives mid-chat.
Why
Keeps accountability with a human and turns near misses and incidents into reportable, fixable events instead of silent mistakes.

Workflow

How
Break a process into steps; for each step record the output and who sees it next. Delegate only steps whose output a named human reviews.
When
Designing a repeatable process, mapping last week's work onto a run map, or checking whether a control still exists.
Why
Makes safe delegation visible and exposes steps that have no checker after them.

Fluency

How
Name the report, reader, frequency, data and format; prototype early and refine from feedback; restart from a summary when context overloads.
When
Requests are vague, the owner is unreachable, or the user can only say what they want after seeing a first version.
Why
Removes ambiguity and rework before an agent acts on the wrong assumption.

Just Delegate It

How
Hand off tasks with clear inputs, outputs and a human checker; keep final decisions and irreversible sends with people.
When
A task repeats, has a gate downstream, and can be bounded by clear instructions.
Why
Gains speed without giving up control over high-stakes outcomes.

General Agents on the Web

How
Use agents with search/read connectors to gather public information and draft outputs, then verify claims against source data.
When
Research, monitoring, or collecting information across sites before a human decides.
Why
Scales information gathering — but “emailed and posted” is only a sentence unless a connector actually did it.

Skills & Connectors

How
Put reusable procedures in Skills, reference facts in Project knowledge, rules in Project instructions; grant Connectors only the access actually used.
When
A procedure recurs, content is stale, or a live system must be read.
Why
Reduces context overload, keeps one source of truth, and limits damage from over-broad permissions.

When

T1 — A

Steps 1 and 3 produce a draft that a named person reviews. Steps 2 and 4 end in a final decision or ledger update with nobody judging afterwards, so they stay human.

T2 — C

North 10,000 ÷ 40 = $250/person; South 7,500 ÷ 25 = $300; East 7,000 ÷ 35 = $200. South is highest, North is 40 of 100, and 24,500 ÷ 100 = $245. Only statement 3 is wrong: East spent $7,000, not $7,500.

T3 — B

Before: 60 × 2.5 = 150 h. After: 60 × (1 + 0.5) = 90 h. Saved = 60 h → 60 × $30 = $1,800 gross. Net = $1,800 − $500 = $1,300. A mistakes the “after” hours for savings; C forgets the tool cost.

T4 — B

Time, hand steps and corrections each have a record. No earlier versions were kept, so revision rounds cannot be verified — it is an estimate.

T5 — B

321 lines − 1 header = 320 rows, so the row count is fine. Lapsed rows carrying amounts can inflate a monthly total; the $350 gap plus “no error” is not proof of correctness. Ask for the rows behind December. Report an incident only if data actually left an approved tool.

Q1 — A

Small financial impact = low stakes; an easy follow-up = reversible. That box needs only a light check. Remember this 2×2 is a practice construct, not a course framework.

Q2 — B

High impact and impossible to undo = high stakes + irreversible. A human must approve before the send; checking afterwards is too late.

Q3 — C

Vague + unreachable owner means you cannot resolve the ambiguity yourself. Write down report, reader, frequency, data and format, and hold the open items for confirmation.

P1 — C

200/800 = 25% (statement 1 ok). 75% × 200 = 150 (2 ok). 150/800 ≈ 19% (4 ok). Non-users were never asked, so no comparison with them is possible — statement 3 is unsupported.

P2 — B

1,240 ÷ 1,300 = 95.38% ≈ 95.4%. A decision must rest on a computed figure with a readable method, not a paragraph saying “about 98%”. A suspiciously round or too-exact headline is a warning sign.

P3 — A

Difference = 4.5 − 3 = 1.5 percentage points. Relative change = 1.5 ÷ 3 = 0.5 = +50%. Percent vs percentage points is the classic trap.

P4 — A

Review cost = 10 × $40 = $400. Net = $2,400 − $900 − $400 = $1,100. Forgetting the review column gives B; forgetting the tool cost gives C.

C1 — A

Reference material such as a site map goes in Project knowledge. A procedure goes in a Skill; a rule goes in Project instructions.

C2 — A

The section order lives in the office's own Skill. Edit and re-upload that Skill — repeating it in chat only patching one conversation.

C3 — B

With the owner unreachable, capture report, reader, frequency, data and format and hold the open items. Guessing risks a polished but wrong deliverable.

C4 — A

Nothing was pasted, so no incident occurred — but the exposure was only avoided by chance. That is a near miss, and it should be reported so the risk is fixed.

C5 — A

20 minutes fits inside the 45-minute window, so send it to the senior editor now. A mandatory gate cannot be skipped to save time.

C6 — A

Without a sending connector, “emailed and posted” is just a claim in a summary. Verify in the inbox/page and do it yourself if needed.

C7 — A

Least privilege means shrinking the connector to the folder actually used. Broad access “just in case” is exactly what least privilege avoids.

C8 — A

Checks must be part of the design with a named human owner. If the only checker leaves and nobody notices, a collaborative step has silently become automated.

C9 — B

When the requirement cannot be described in advance, build a small first version, show it, gather feedback and refine: build → show → refine.

C10 — B

Connector actions are limited to search/read. Claude drafts the message; a human performs the send.
